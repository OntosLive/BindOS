# Channels and Transition Cost

BindOS separates three independent axes.

## Logical type

Logical type answers: **what does this operator act on?**

## Communication channel

Channel answers: **through what physical or social medium does the difference arrive?**

Canonical channels include verbal language, text, prosody, facial expression, gaze, gesture, posture, proximity, touch, silence, timing, group response, environmental constraints, institutional rules, and algorithmic signals.

A channel does not determine logical type.

## Transition cost

A move can be formally permitted while remaining functionally expensive.

BindOS represents channel influence as:

> ΔC = sign × magnitude × conductance × gain

The kernel distinguishes actual cost from expected cost.

Expected sanction matters because a transition may become expensive before any punishment occurs.

## Presence versus text

Text often removes gaze, proximity, posture, prosody, and immediate timing pressure. It also introduces response latency.

Therefore identical verbal content can produce different transition fields.

## Charisma and authority

BindOS does not require charisma as a primitive trait.

It can first be decomposed into channel intensity, conductance, gain, group reinforcement, status, and the resulting change in transition costs.
